# JOUR 201: Reporting and Writing I — Syllabus

## Course overview

This course teaches the core skills of news reporting: finding stories, interviewing sources, verifying facts, and writing clear, accurate copy on deadline. You will report and write six stories during the quarter, from a short news brief to a 900-word feature. Class meets Tuesdays and Thursdays from 2:00 to 3:20 p.m. in Fisk Hall, Room 217.

## Instructor and office hours

Your instructor holds office hours on Wednesdays from 10:00 a.m. to noon and by appointment. Book a slot through the course calendar at least 24 hours ahead. The teaching assistant holds drop-in hours on Mondays from 4:00 to 5:30 p.m. in the newsroom on the second floor. Email questions go to the course inbox, and we answer within one business day. Questions sent after 6 p.m. on a Friday are answered on Monday.

## Required materials

You need a copy of the current AP Stylebook (the online edition is fine), a notebook for interviews, and a recording app on your phone. You must ask permission before recording any interview. There is no textbook to buy; all readings are posted on the course site.

## Assignments and grading

Your grade is made up of six story assignments (60 percent), weekly AP style quizzes (15 percent), a beat memo (10 percent), and participation in story meetings and peer edits (15 percent). The final story, a 900-word feature, counts for 20 of the 60 story points.

Every story must include at least two named human sources you interviewed yourself. Press releases, social media posts, and other news outlets do not count as sources. A story with fewer than two interviewed sources cannot earn more than a C.

## Deadlines and late work

Stories are due at 11:59 p.m. on the due date, submitted as a Google Doc shared with the instructor. Late stories lose one letter grade for each day they are late. In journalism, a missed deadline means the story does not run, so plan your reporting early. If you have an emergency, contact the instructor before the deadline, not after. Each student gets one 48-hour extension for the quarter, which you can use on any story except the final feature.

## Fact errors

Accuracy is the foundation of this course. A factual error in a published story is an automatic F on that assignment. Fact errors include a misspelled name, a wrong title, a wrong date, a wrong number, or a quote attributed to the wrong person. You can recover half of the lost points by filing a correction memo within 48 hours that explains how the error happened and how you will prevent it.

## AP style quizzes

Quizzes happen every Thursday at the start of class and take ten minutes. Each quiz has 15 questions on the week's style topics. Your lowest quiz score is dropped. If you miss a quiz for an excused absence, you can take it during the TA's drop-in hours that week.

## Attendance

You may miss two classes for any reason without penalty. Each additional unexcused absence lowers your participation grade by five points. Story meetings on Tuesdays count double, because your classmates depend on your feedback.

## Academic integrity

Plagiarism and fabrication end careers in journalism and will result in failing the course. Fabrication includes inventing a source, inventing a quote, or combining several people into one composite character. You may use AI tools to check grammar, but every sentence of reporting must come from your own interviews and documents. If you use an AI tool, add a note at the end of your story saying which tool and what you used it for.
